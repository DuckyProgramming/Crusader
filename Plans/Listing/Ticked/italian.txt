Most Italian units in Africa were destroyed in some way or another during Compass. The above units in Europe were transported in to partially replace the losses.
The only surviving units were the greatly depleted Sirte Division and the 21st Light Tank Battalion. Efforts were made to rebuild both of them.
The Sirte Division would take some time to refill its depleted infantry battalions, but leftover frontier guard artillery was reformed into its artillery support.
The 21st Light Tank Battalion was reequipped with nine M13/40 medium tanks and was renumbered as the 52nd.

During Operation Crusader, the Savona Division and all its attached elements were destroyed in full, as were the few remaining tanks of the 52nd Medium Tank Battalion.
However, the Sirte Division was once again operational, refreshed by remaining Savona troops, and was sent to the front.
Also, during the deployment to this battle, two artillery battalions lost their guns on transport to Africa. They were reformed as the Raggruppamento Batterie Volanti.

The results of El Alamein had cost the Sirte Division half of its infantry strength. For the second time the division was near destroyed. This time its remnants went to the Trento.
On the bright side, this freed up a set of divisional support units to give to RECo, upgrading it to a full armored division.
Unluckily, its assigned tank battalion, the 52nd, had already been wiped out, so instead the armored cars of the Romolo Gessi Police Battalion were reformed into a full armored battalion.
The Raggruppamento Batterie Volanti (now converted to the 136th Artillery Regiment) was reinforced with two additional groups formed from the 43rd Artillery Regiment.
