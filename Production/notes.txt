To reduce the confusion listed above about the state of the Guardia alla Frontiera at Tobruk, I'll compile what I know here:
1. The sectors responsible for the town were the 31st and 32nd. A few stragglers from elsewhere might've been but aren't recorded.
The sectors were intended to contain 1800 men each. However it appears only 2300 men actually showed up for the defense.
The XXI or XXXI Libyan Battalions may have been there, but it is entirely unclear which one or if either was actually there at all. These also might not be part of the GaF.
The 31st had 10 artillery groups and the 32nd had 12. This disagrees with Niehorster but I belive the mistake is over there.
The 31st had an engineer company that was surely real but might not have been in Tobruk when the battle began. The 32nd also had an engineer company but I cannot locate it.

If I had to guess, the 1800 men number was entirely theoretical. No sources indicate that anywhere near this number of infantry ever showed up (but maybe it includes artillerymen?)
The 2300 men seems not entirely unreasonable. If you include the 31st GaF Infantry Battalion, 21st/31st Libyan Coastal Battalion, both engineer companies, and lesser support, it about adds up.
It's probably also the 31st and not the 21st Battalion. Montanari is far from infallible and this type of mistake isn't uncommon, plus 31 is the number of the sector it's in.
The eastern sector was split into sub-sectors A and B. As we know from the western sector, 1 battalion manned each sub-sector, so there should've been two battalions worth of troops there.
In this case, I/69 or II/69 (the other was probably destroyed or depleted) defended one while the 31st Frontier Guard Infantry Battalion defended the other.
The Libyan battalion is supposedly "coastal" so I don't think it would've ended up deployed on the outer ring; it would've been on, well, the coast. I also don't know its structure or even its number.
