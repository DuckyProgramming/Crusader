I've been looking at various other bridging phases of the campaign in North Africa. One of them is the sort of "lull" while Rommel is arriving in Tripoli.

1. On page 182, he mentions the "1 batteria mista del I gruppo del 22o raggruppamento" as part of the artillery complement of one sector for the defense. No elements of the 22o raggruppamento are described in the other sectors. No further mentions of this unit are made in this chapter.

2. On page 332, a report from XXII Corps Chief of Staff Carlo Rostagno mentions the 22o Raggruppamento consisting of "1o gruppo da 100/17, 1a batteria da 105/28" and some support, anti-aircraft, and provisional units.

The GaF artillery in Europe had a similar structure. Artillery regiments existed to serve as depots but in combat the artillery would be subordinate to the sector/strongpoint/fortification commands.
It appears that some fortifications were classified based on the types and number of guns contained within, but this was discontinued or didn't apply to Africa because those units do not fit into this schema.
This was later changed when the GaF's artillery was merged with existing static artillery. Now medium and small guns could be added with numbers varying from 3-8 for small and 3-6 for medium, though this still didn't apply to African units.
Some batteries were denoted as SP, "sempre pronte", or "always ready". This wasn't really the case though, as these "always ready" batteries took several hours to activate. Still, they were better than regular batteries that could take days.
The average artillery regiment prior to their dissolution down to depots included around 10 or 20 such sempre pronte batteries. None have been found in Africa.

GaF engineer units were truly mixed in nature, including both the men responsible for maintaining field fortifications, as well as those responsible for maintianing electricity, lights, and communication lines.
